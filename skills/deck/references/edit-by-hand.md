# Stage 6 — edit by hand

**This happens in the editor, not through you.**

Editing AI writing by hand is what gives a deck a real human voice — theirs. And
that voice is what the audience is actually assessing. A deck the founder has been
over line by line reads differently from one they approved in a chat window, even
when the words are similar.

So your job at this stage is small and specific:

1. **Send them into the editor**, on the deck (`get_deck` returns `edit_url`).
2. **Name the slides most likely to read as AI-written** — the ones where you supplied
   the phrasing rather than them. You know which those are: you wrote them. Three or
   four, not a list of twenty.
3. **Name the lines you'd change first** — the abstract ones, the ones that would fit
   any company if you swapped the name out, the ones where their own words from
   `The story` were better than what ended up on the slide.
4. **Get out of the way.** Do not offer to do it for them. That is the one thing this
   stage is for.

If they ask you to make an edit anyway, make it — but keep their phrasing, and say
which line you changed.

## What not to do

- Don't re-run QC hoping to catch it. The generic-AI-voice problem is not a lint; it
  is why this stage is a human one.
- Don't tell them the deck is finished. **They** finish it.
