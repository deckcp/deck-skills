# Anti-AI Aesthetics — patterns to reject unless the source explicitly uses them

These are not universally forbidden. They are forbidden as **defaults**.

## Common generic patterns

- three or four equal rounded cards for unrelated content
- icon-in-circle + title + paragraph repeated on most slides
- random gradient blobs / glows
- purple-on-dark "AI startup" palettes without brand evidence
- cream + editorial serif + terracotta as a generic "premium" shortcut
- decorative title underlines
- full-width colored title bars without a system reason
- thin edge stripes added only to make the slide feel designed
- excessive pills / badges
- oversized numerals used on every slide regardless of content
- arbitrary 01 / 02 / 03 numbering
- excessive rounded corners
- centered text inside every shape
- stock metaphor icons: rockets, lightbulbs, handshakes, targets
- stock corporate photography as a substitute for real product / people / places
- one dark slab with a glow behind every slide
- equal-weight rainbow accent colors
- faux dashboards for content that is not actually a dashboard

## Rewrite the instinct

Instead of:

```text
Need visual interest → add more objects.
```

Use:

```text
Need visual interest → strengthen composition, hierarchy, crop, scale, spacing,
or the relationship between text and evidence.
```

## Generic-copy warning

Visual genericness often comes with generic writing. Flag phrases such as:

- seamless
- unlock
- empower
- next-generation
- cutting-edge
- revolutionize
- leverage (when a simpler verb works)

Do not rewrite factual or branded language merely to avoid a word; flag only empty filler.

## Over-visualization is also a generic-AI smell (v0.8.2)

AI decks fail in two opposite directions: **too much text**, and **every slide forced into an
infographic**. Both read as generated. Avoid both. Beyond the patterns above, treat these as
over-visualization smells to reject unless the content genuinely earns them:

- a diagram drawn for a pure statement or positioning line
- a bespoke icon system built just because a slide has three or four items
- icons that merely duplicate the adjacent text
- redundant lines, containers, or labels around content that reads fine without them
- many consecutive `visual-led` slides with no typographic pause
- a chart / diagram / icon set / photo added only to satisfy a media-mix category

A professional designer knows when *not* to add something. Restraint is part of creativity —
see `visual-storytelling.md` (Visual Restraint & Rhythm) for the gate and the remove-before-adding pass.
